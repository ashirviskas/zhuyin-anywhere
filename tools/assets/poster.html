Zhuyin Anywhere

A browser add-on for reading Taiwanese websites while learning Mandarin. It writes the pronunciation beside every Chinese character, and tells you what words mean when you point at them.

The two characters on the left say “zhùyīn”, the name of the pronunciation symbols themselves.

How to read the little symbols

A Chinese character doesn’t tell you how to say it. Children in Taiwan learn with zhuyin, also called bopomofo: 37 sound symbols written in a small column right beside each character. Read the column from top to bottom, then apply the tone mark.

銀

Together: yín, meaning “silver”.

The tone changes the word

Mandarin has four tones and a light one. Same sound, different pitch, different meaning:

嗎

No mark means high and flat. ˊ rises, ˇ dips and comes back up, ˋ falls. A dot above the column means short and light.

Zhuyin on every page

Turn it on and any website written in Traditional Chinese (Taiwan’s script) gets zhuyin next to each character: articles, menus, headlines, YouTube comments.

The page itself is untouched otherwise. Copy text and you get clean Chinese, without the symbols mixed in.

Point at a word to see what it means

The card shows the word with its zhuyin in large print and a speaker button that reads it aloud. Then the English for the whole word, and what each character means on its own. The one under your mouse is highlighted.

Here, “pearls + milk + tea” is bubble tea. The character under the cursor means bead.

Select a sentence to translate it

Highlight any text and a box shows the English translation, plus a word-by-word breakdown for short phrases.

Each word in the list has its zhuyin in blue and its own speaker button; the one at the bottom reads the whole selection. Pinyin can be switched on in the settings.

The card and the translation box follow the system's light or dark setting, or can be set to either in the settings.

It reads words, not just characters

Some characters are pronounced differently depending on the word they’re in. The add-on looks at whole words, so it picks the right reading. Look at the same character 行 in two words:

行人

xíng-rén: pedestrian. Here 行 is xíng, “to walk”.

銀行

yín-háng: bank. Here 行 is háng, “a business”.

Pop-ups and buttons stay usable

Zhuyin makes text about 40% wider. Small boxes like newsletter pop-ups, buttons and sticky headers weren’t built for that, so there the symbols are hidden. Point at a word and they appear in the card instead.

Compact mode

The text fits again. The page’s article text keeps its zhuyin.

Controls

Everything else lives in the add-on’s menu: turn it on or off for each website, move the symbols above the characters instead of beside them, and choose the translation service.

Alt + Z

Show or hide the zhuyin on the page.

Alt + T

Translate the selected text when automatic translation is off.

Esc

Close the translation box or word card.

Menu

Turn it on or off for this site, change the layout, pick the translation service.

A Violentmonkey userscript for Firefox and Chrome. Pronunciations follow Taiwan’s standard, using the McBopomofo dictionary from the yt-zhuyin project. Word meanings come from CC-CEDICT (CC BY-SA 4.0, MDBG).
